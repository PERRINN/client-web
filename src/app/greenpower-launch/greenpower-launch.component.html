An open letter from Nico Perrin

01 / A season to build on

Our first year. A serious starting point.

In 2026, we took Baby 424 into its first Greenpower F24+ season. We finished as vice-champions, with four podiums. PERRINN Limited invested £27,000 to turn the design into a car, take it racing and learn what it could do.

I’m proud of that beginning. Now I want to open the next chapter to more people: companies, universities, students, independent engineers, manufacturers and contributors with skills beyond engineering.

02 / The ambition

A faster car. A wider team.

2026 baseline36 mph
→
2027 target40 mph

For 2027, our goal is to reach an average race speed of 40 mph and contend for the championship. That is a target we have to earn through engineering, testing and reliable race execution.

I also want us to demonstrate something larger: that a coordinated, open network can achieve top-level motorsport engineering, while giving more people meaningful access to a racing team.

03 / Where we can improve

Real engineering. Open challenges.

Make every kilogram count.

Our car is currently 67 kg. I want us to work towards the low-60-kg range, examining the structure, components and manufacturing choices without compromising safety or reliability.

A lighter steering assembly.

We already have a scheme for a much lighter steering assembly. It needs further development, manufacturing and validation to take it from a promising design to a working assembly on the car.

Find more in the motor.

Reversing the motor’s rotation offers a power and efficiency opportunity. We need to engineer the necessary changes, make the parts and validate the result. An opportunity becomes useful only when it works on the car.

Stronger wheels. A more reliable car.

A weakness in our wheel design led to a crash during the Goodwood heat in May 2026. It was an important lesson: making the car more robust and reliable is a central part of our development work.

We have a new solid-wheel design ready for refinement and manufacturing. I’m looking for contributors to help develop it, make the parts and validate the assembly before it goes racing.

Take the pipeline to the car.

Several developments are already in the pipeline. They need people to take responsibility for progressing them through refinement, manufacturing, assembly and workshop validation, so the car is ready when testing starts.

A hardware proposal needs a practical manufacturing plan: who will build it, and who will deliver ready-to-assemble parts? If you have an idea but no fabrication access, partner with someone who does.

Help from the driving seat.

I’m looking for lighter drivers aged 18–23. The F24+ category has a maximum age of 25, and candidates must meet the competition’s eligibility requirements.

Our races are primarily in the south of the UK. Goodwood and Castle Combe are key venues, along with Mallory Park in the Midlands. Drivers must be able to travel independently to the selected races.

04 / Work in the open

Start with what we already know.

Our existing designs and 2026 race data are public. I want you to use that foundation: examine the design, challenge our assumptions and bring evidence to the discussion.

05 / How we will work

Bring an idea. Build it together.

  1. Discuss
  2. Develop
  3. Manufacture
  4. Nico approval
  5. Validate
  6. Race

We will discuss and coordinate every initiative through PERRINN.com, supported by online meetings. I will oversee the initiatives and approve parts before we use them. Bring proposals into the discussion early so we can agree the direction before manufacturing.

Contributions of time, materials and manufacturing capacity are voluntary and in kind. PERRINN will not fund manufacturing of new development parts. The invitation is to contribute what you can, and to collaborate where a project needs more than one set of skills.

Our workshop near Bath and Bristol will remain the base for assembly, motor and battery testing, and validation. PERRINN will handle event logistics and race organisation.

We will record each contribution on PERRINN.com and recognise participating companies and universities on the car. Contributors are welcome at races: I want the people who help develop this car to be part of its story.
